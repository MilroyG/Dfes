What Are Landslides?

A landslide is the movement of a mass of rock, debris, or earth down a slope under the influence of gravity. They are often triggered by factors such as heavy rainfall, earthquakes, volcanic activity, human activities like construction and deforestation, or natural erosion processes. Landslides can vary in size, speed, and the amount of material displaced, causing damage to infrastructure, property, and sometimes resulting in loss of life.

  • Landslides in Goa

Why Goa is Prone to Landslides:

  • Topography: The hilly terrain of the Western Ghats and certain elevated regions in Goa are vulnerable to landslides.
  • Heavy Monsoon Rains: Goa experiences intense and prolonged rainfall during the monsoon season, increasing soil saturation and reducing slope stability.
  • Deforestation: Clearing vegetation for construction, mining, and tourism infrastructure weakens soil cohesion.
  • Unregulated Construction: Building roads, hotels, and houses on unstable slopes without proper geotechnical studies exacerbates the risk.

Mining Activities: Extensive mining has disturbed the natural geological stability in certain regions of Goa.

Dangers of Landslides

  • Loss of Life and Injuries: Fast-moving landslides can bury people, homes, and vehicles.
  • Destruction of Property: Homes, roads, bridges, and agricultural land can be destroyed or rendered useless.
  • 3Blocked Transport Routes: Roads and railway lines may be obstructed by fallen debris.
  • Disruption of Essential Services: Power lines, water supply systems, and communication networks can be damaged.
  • Environmental Impact: Landslides can result in soil erosion, loss of fertile land, and habitat destruction.

Economic Losses: Recovery and rebuilding efforts are expensive and time-consuming.

Know Your Risk

Assessing Your Landslide Risk:

Slope Stability: Understand if your home or workplace is located on or near a steep slope.

Drainage Patterns: Poor drainage or water pooling near slopes increases landslide risk.

Past Landslide History: Check historical records of landslides in your area.

Soil and Rock Conditions: Loose, sandy soil or fractured rock layers are more prone to sliding.

Signs of Potential Landslides:

Cracks in walls, floors, or ground

Tilting trees or poles

Sudden changes in water flow or muddy water springs

Vulnerable Structures:

Houses built on steep or unstable slopes

Buildings near riverbanks or mountain edges

Landslides Alerts and Warnings

Types of Alerts:

  • Watch: Conditions are favourable for landslides; stay alert.
  • Warning: Landslide has occurred or is imminent in your area.
  • Evacuation Order: Immediate evacuation is necessary due to high risk.

Issued by Authorities:

  • India Meteorological Department (IMD) issues rainfall-related alerts.
  • State Disaster Management Authority (SDMA) provides region-specific alerts.
  • Local authorities and district administrations share updates via SMS, apps, and public announcements.
  • How to Stay Informed:
  1. Weather Forecasts: Monitor IMD weather reports regularly.
  2. Official Websites: Follow updates from SDMA and local government portals.
  3. Mobile Alerts: Subscribe to disaster alert services via SMS or mobile apps.
  4. Social Media: Follow verified government social media handles for real-time updates.
  5. Community Programs: Participate in local disaster preparedness programs.

Dos and Don’ts During a Landslides

Before a Landslide:

  • Dos:
  • Stay informed about weather updates.
  • Identify evacuation routes and safe zones.
  • Ensure your home has proper drainage systems.
  • Keep an emergency kit ready (first aid, torch, bottled water, essential documents).

  • Don’t:
  • Ignore early warning signs like cracks or unusual ground movement.
  • Build homes on steep, unstable slopes without professional consultation.
  • Block natural drainage systems with debris or construction waste.

After a Landslide:

  • Dos:
  • Stay away from landslide-affected zones until authorities declare them safe.
  • Report broken utility lines or damaged infrastructure.
  • Assist others if you are safe and capable.
  • Follow instructions from disaster management authorities.

    • Don’t:
  • Re-enter your home until it has been inspected for structural safety.
  • Drink water from damaged supply lines without boiling or treating it.

  • Conclusion:
  • Preparedness is Key!
  • Attend landslide awareness workshops organized by local authorities.
  • Install early warning systems in high-risk areas.
  • Plant deep-rooted vegetation to stabilize slopes.
  • Be proactive in identifying and mitigating risks in your community.
